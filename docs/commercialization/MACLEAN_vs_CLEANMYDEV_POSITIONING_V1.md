# MACLEAN vs CleanMyDev — Differentiated Competition V1.0

## 1. Executive decision

Do **not** position maclean as “a better CleanMyDev”.

Position:

> **maclean — Developer Storage Intelligence & Automation**

One-line distinction:

> CleanMyDev cleans developer storage. maclean explains, monitors, governs, cleans and automates developer storage.

## 2. Competitive facts

CleanMyDev currently markets:
- 110+ developer-specific cleanup targets
- Xcode, Cursor, Ollama, Claude Code, Codex, Cline, Docker, npm/pnpm/Bun and related categories
- free read-only audit
- review-before-delete
- Trash by default
- no subscription / lifetime purchase
- $9.99 launch pricing moving toward $29.99

Storage Cleaner also targets developers and AI models, with project grouping, last-use information, automation and low-cost lifetime pricing.

Therefore the following are **table stakes**, not differentiation:
- Xcode scanner
- Docker scanner
- AI model scanner
- node_modules scanner
- Trash
- Undo
- offline/local processing
- no telemetry
- lifetime license
- “developer cleaner” wording

## 3. New category

### Category
**Developer Storage Intelligence**

### Job to be done
“When my Mac is filling up, tell me what is happening, what matters, what is safe to reclaim, and prevent the same problem from silently returning.”

### Product loop
Observe → Explain → Classify → Decide → Clean → Recover → Automate → Forecast

## 4. Strategic wedge

### Wedge 1 — Regenerable Data
Classify data into:
- Active
- Regenerable
- Downloadable
- Stale
- User-owned
- Destructive / irreversible
- Unknown

### Wedge 2 — Project Intelligence
Group storage by project instead of only by tool/path.

### Wedge 3 — Growth Intelligence
Show storage deltas over time:
- +38 GB this week
- top growth contributors
- projected full date

### Wedge 4 — Policy
Allow developers to define safe cleanup rules.

### Wedge 5 — Automation
Scheduled, dry-run-first, auditable cleanup.

### Wedge 6 — CLI / CI
Treat maclean-core as a headless storage intelligence engine.

## 6. What NOT to do

1. Do not advertise “150+ rules” as the primary differentiator.
2. Do not undercut CleanMyDev to $4.99.
3. Do not build a subscription-only plan.
4. Do not copy their landing page structure or wording.
5. Do not make “AI cleaner” the product category.
6. Do not let AI directly delete files.

## 7. North-star metric

**Healthy Reclaimable Storage (HRS)**:
> the amount of storage maclean can confidently classify as reclaimable without touching active/user-owned data.

Secondary metrics:
- scan → first insight conversion
- free → cleanup conversion
- cleanup success rate
- restore rate
- policy adoption
- 7/30-day storage growth detected
- false-positive safety incidents: target zero

## 8. Product moat

The moat is not paths. Paths are copyable.

The moat is a continuously improving local knowledge model:

Entity → Project → Tool → Artifact → Lifecycle → Usage → Regenerability → Risk → Action

## 9. Competitive messaging

Do not say:
> “Better than CleanMyDev.”

Say:
> “Clean your developer Mac once. Understand and control developer storage every day.”

## 10. Acceptance criteria

A user opening maclean should answer within 30 seconds:
1. What is consuming my storage?
2. How much is developer storage?
3. How much is reclaimable?
4. Why is it safe to remove?
5. What grew recently?
6. What should I do next?
